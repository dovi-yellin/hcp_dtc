**Figure S3. Nonlinear dependence of simulated fALFF on recurrent gain at τ = 120 ms.**

Simulations used random recurrent networks with N = 400 units, connection probability p = 0.2 and neuronal time constant τ = 120 ms. Activity was observed through the 1.3-s effective filter used in the revised Figure 4. Points show mean fALFF across 14 independent network-and-input realizations, after averaging four 300-sample observation windows within each realization; error bars indicate SEM across the 14 realization means.

**(A)** Mean fALFF over the fitted gain interval, G = 0.930–0.994 (actual distance to instability G′ = 1 − G = 0.006–0.07). The solid curve shows the existing power-law approximation, $\mathrm{fALFF} \approx 0.1928(1-G)^{-0.2401}$, obtained by ordinary least-squares regression of log₁₀(mean fALFF) on log₁₀(G′), with equal weight for the eight simulated gain levels in this interval (log-space R² = 0.974).

**(B)** The same ensemble over the full simulated range, G = 0.700–0.997 (G′ = 0.003–0.3). Shading and orange points identify the interval fitted in A; gray points lie outside that interval. The solid segment repeats the same fitted curve, and dotted segments show its extrapolation, without refitting. The broader simulation curve departs from this finite-range approximation, showing flattening toward both ends of the sampled range. The approximation is descriptive; it does not establish universal critical scaling or replace the numerical calibration used for the cortical map. This figure presents the stationary ensemble mean rather than the three-realization sampling display in main Figure 4C.
